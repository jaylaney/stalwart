## Accepted residuals and inherited behavior

- The default-role epoch line was adopted without an isolating test or mutation. Upstream's
  wipe tests exercise separate paths. The epoch bump narrows stale reuse. An inherited
  spill-cache loader can still publish an oversized old-permission token after invalidation
  and before the post-insert eviction.
- Codec qualification recoded all eligible blobs across accounts and back. Temporary-only
  blobs were expired and purged while the key was still loaded. Whole-store decoded reads
  were checked before and after key retirement.
- iTIP retains upstream's high-SEQUENCE override behavior. A full REQUEST removes absent
  overrides only through its master SEQUENCE; an override at a higher sequence can remain. The
  ordinary receive path and key-account drain share this reducer. Explicit CANCEL rules remain
  covered separately.
- `BlobRecode` can race purge between its commit-marker check and payload replacement and may
  recreate an unlinked ordinary blob. This operator-triggered recode/purge window is accepted.
- A default-role edit can trigger an Argon2 burst while affected credentials refresh.
- A non-success HTTP response to a request with a body sets `Connection: close`. Subsequent
  requests on a new TLS connection incur another handshake.
- The configured outbound gateway recipient-key fingerprint is traced beside queue-event addresses.
- The guards at `tests/src/jmap/mail/query_changes.rs:255–257` and in the key-mode mirror
  still skip the inherited `collapseThreads` case. The new key-account control in
  `tests/src/za/mail/search.rs` exercises collapsed query changes and checks that a reply and
  its parent keep singleton thread IDs.
- Server-side blob encryption is outside the zero-access guarantee. Backups carry decoded blob
  payloads and swap snapshots use plain codec settings.

The inherited iTIP override and BlobRecode/purge behaviors are accepted upstream residuals.
